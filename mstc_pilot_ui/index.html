Study information

We are testing whether a mathematical score (MST-C) predicts which explanation heatmaps people find legible. You will compare pairs of heatmaps and answer a few short questions about your background and your impressions.

We record your choices, response times, a few interaction events (e.g. if the browser tab was hidden), your screen size and your questionnaire answers. We do not record your name or e-mail. Data are stored under a random participant code and used for research only. Participation is voluntary and you can stop at any time by closing the tab.

Step 1 of 2 · What you'll see

A photo and two heatmaps

On every page you'll see a photo at the top. Below it are two heatmaps of that same photo: colour overlays that highlight certain parts of it. The heatmaps may look abstract, and the two may look very different from each other.

Every page looks like this. Only the photo and the heatmaps change.

You do not need to understand what the heatmaps show or what they mean. Please do not try to interpret them.

Step 2 of 2 · Your task

Choose the heatmap that's easier to read

Your task is to choose the heatmap you find easier to read and more visually appealing. Go with your first impression — there are no right or wrong answers.

  • Click directly on the heatmap you prefer, or press ← / → for left / right.
  • Your choice is saved immediately and the next pair appears. There is no "Next" button, and you cannot go back.
  • The same photo may appear again with different heatmaps. Judge each new pair on its own, regardless of your earlier choices.
  • There are 172 pairs in total, with a short break halfway.
  • A few quality checks are included, so please stay attentive until the end.

You'll start with 2 practice pairs (they don't count), right after a couple of quick questions about these instructions. Please do the study in one go, with this tab in the foreground.
